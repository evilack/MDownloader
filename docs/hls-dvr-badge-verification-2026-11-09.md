# DVR source badge — 2026-11-09

HLS sources with confirmed retained live history now display **DVR** in place of **HLS**. The `LIVE` badge remains separate. Transport IDs stay `M3U8`, so download, permission and current-start behavior are unchanged. Popup and panel candidates use the same component; persisted jobs and partial-save jobs also carry the DVR source capability.

## Display policy

HLS does not supply a universal DVR flag. The display policy is deliberately independent of hostname and URL spelling:

- A currently live `EXT-X-PLAYLIST-TYPE:EVENT` manifest qualifies. EVENT's append-only semantics are described in [RFC 8216 §4.3.3.5](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8216#section-4.3.3.5).
- Otherwise, a currently live media manifest qualifies when its retained complete-segment duration is at least **120 seconds** and at least **10 target durations**. This is an application display heuristic for a substantial rewind window, not a threshold defined by the HLS specification or a guarantee of full-broadcast retention.
- Short rolling buffers, explicit VOD and ended media remain HLS. A master alone does not establish DVR support. Classification may therefore remain HLS until a qualifying media response is available, and some short or unmarked DVR windows will not qualify.

Observed media capability is tied to the exact document and declared master/rendition relationship, retained across later response-header observations, and sent through the public scan response. Candidate rows update if capability arrives later. Running jobs upgrade when refreshed playlists first establish DVR capability; the source capability then stays with the job after save/termination. A stale job's `false` cannot hide a newly confirmed candidate's `true`.

A paused sole Blob/native-HLS player may have only loaded its master. If exactly one current-document master exists and its media capability is unknown, one declared rendition is checked through the existing host-permission and document/page-bound observation path. No video segment is requested. The metadata probe has a **5-second timeout**, shares pending work, retries failures no more often than every 30 seconds, and reuses successful media metadata. Multiple masters are left unprobed, including the mixed known/unknown case. General declared-source probe callers keep their existing timeout.
